KSU Launches Digital Arang To Protect Kalinga
Indigenous Knowledge In Al Systems

TINGLAYAN, Kalinga — Kalinga State University (KSU) launched the Digital Arang on October 30, 2026,
in Bangad, Tinglayan, advancing a community-governed digital repository intended to preserve and
protect Kalinga Indigenous Knowledge Systems and Practices (IKSP), including in digital environments
involving artificial intelligence (Al).

The launch took place during the province’s 2026 Indigenous Peoples Month celebration and the 29th
commemoration of the Indigenous Peoples’ Rights Act (IPRA), under the theme “Kalinga’s Voice, Our only
choice, shaping our destiny, sustaining our peace, standing for true autonomy.” Digital Arang is a
collaboration among KSU, the Office of the Provincial Indigenous Peoples Mandatory Representative
(IPMR), and the Kalinga Bodong Council of Elders (KBCE), with the New York University Peace Research
and Education Program (NYU PREP) serving as consultant. KSU manages the repository, while the
Office of the Provincial IPMR administers its funds, programs, and activities.

The initiative is anchored in the Kalinga Digital Arang Ordinance, authored by Provincial IPMR Michael
Karel B. Sugguiyao and signed into provincial policy by Governor James S. Edduba in September 2026.
The ordinance establishes the Kalinga IKSP artificial intelligence database platform called “Arang” and
provides a framework for ethical, culturally grounded, and rights-based Al governance in the province.

The repository’s framework provides four tiers of access to Indigenous knowledge, distinguishing
information that may be publicly available from knowledge requiring greater protection. These safeguards
recognize that access to cultural knowledge must take account of community rights and the nature of the
information, particularly where sacred or restricted knowledge is concerned. °
The framework also provides for free, prior, and informed consent in the use of Kalinga IKSP by Al

Oversight Committee is designated to govern the repository and oversee implementation of the
ordinance. Its guiding customary principles include paniyaw, ngilin, ba-in, and attom.

The initiative seeks to place Indigenous communities’ authority over their knowledge at the center of
digital governance. Rather than treating cultural information as material freely available for collection and
reuse, Digital Arang establishes safeguards intended to recognize the rights of knowledge holders to
determine how their knowledge is accessed, used, and protected. KSU and the Office of the Provincial
IPMR previously presented Digital Arang at side events of the 81st United Nations General Assembly in
New York City from September 18 to 28, 2026. The presentation brought the province’s approach to
Indigenous data governance to an international audience.

Through Digital Arang, KSU and its partners seek to strengthen Indigenous data sovereignty and protect
Kalinga cultural knowledge as digital technologies continue to expand. The launching was led by KSU
President Dr. Joy Grace P. Doctor, Twinkle A. Bautista, Marilou D. Alngag, and Agnes Balawan. The other
proponents were Desiree Kate B. Alngag, Fortune Mae S. Baguiwan, Precious A. Tanding, Renclyn L.
Yapan, and Xin-Ziyi O. Gup-ay.
